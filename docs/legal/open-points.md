# Legal open points — internal

Not a published document. What a Lithuanian-qualified lawyer and the accountant have to
confirm before [the advisor agreement](advisor-agreement/v1.md) and
[the Checkpoint terms](checkpoint-purchase-terms/v1.md) go live, and the contradictions those two
documents surfaced in what is already published.

## Contradictions with what is already published

1. **Two operators — decided: the operator is the Lithuanian VšĮ.** The new agreements are
   right; [terms-of-use.md](terms-of-use/v1.md) and [refund-policy.md](refund-policy/v1.md)
   are behind. They still name the Georgian individual entrepreneur (code 306422564) as
   the operator, keep Lithuania as a representative office, and choose **Georgian**
   governing law. Both need to be reissued with the VšĮ as the operator and Lithuanian
   law, together with the contact block and the courts clause.
2. **The money and the contract are in different entities.** The Paddle account is
   registered to the Georgian individual entrepreneur, so the buyer pays into that entity
   while the Checkpoint terms say they contract with the VšĮ, and Paddle's own verification
   requires the payout account to match the seller's legal identity exactly.

   **Decided: the Checkpoint is billed on the VšĮ's own Stripe account.** The seller, the
   contract, the invoice and the money are then one entity, and it sidesteps point 5
   entirely. Paddle stays where it already works — vacancies, courses, events — under the
   Georgian entity until that is moved separately.

   What changes because Stripe is a processor and not a merchant of record:
   - **We are the seller of record.** We issue the invoice, in our own name and numbering,
     under Lithuanian invoicing rules. Nobody does it for us.
   - **We determine and remit VAT ourselves.** See point 3 — the destination rules for
     virtual training are not what a first reading suggests.
   - **We carry the chargebacks and the disputes**, and the fraud screening decisions.
   - Stripe does not return its processing fee on a refund either, so the deduction in
     clause 4.1 of the Checkpoint terms still has something to deduct.
3. **The dead ODR link.** Terms of Use §17.4 points consumers to the European
   Commission's ODR platform, which stopped operating on 20 July 2025. Replace with the
   VVTAT reference used in the Checkpoint terms.
4. **Age.** The Terms of Use admit users from 16. A Checkpoint is a paid contract and the
   drafts require 18. Checkout has to enforce it.
5. **Refund policy.** The Checkpoint's 7-day rule is not in
   [refund-policy.md](refund-policy/v1.md). Add a Checkpoint section there or point that
   policy at these terms.

## To confirm with counsel

1. **Economic activity of the VšĮ.** That selling Checkpoints and paid consultations fits
   the statutory goals in the articles, and that the profit-distribution ban is respected
   when curators are paid fees.
2. **The founder's arrangement with the VšĮ is a separate track**, settled elsewhere and
   deliberately not described in either agreement — the author contracts with the VšĮ for
   the whole program and nothing about the internal arrangement belongs in a consumer
   document. The only thing these documents depend on: **the VšĮ must hold the right to run
   the program on gdhub.io before the first sale.**
3. **Payments to individuals.** A fee paid to a Lithuanian resident who has no individual
   activity certificate obliges the payer to withhold personal income tax and social
   contributions. The draft requires the advisor to hold a lawful basis, and lets us
   withhold where the law demands it — confirm the wording is enough, and what documents
   we must collect and keep.
4. **VAT — and it turns on what the Checkpoint actually is.** The product decision is that
   this is **not a training course**: nobody is taught a syllabus, the team works on its own
   project and is advised on it. That reading is what the program page and these terms
   describe, and it has to stay consistent everywhere, because the tax treatment follows the
   description.
   - **As advisory services, the place of supply is the ordinary one.** For a consumer
     anywhere in the EU that is where we are established — **Lithuania**, under Article 45
     — so Lithuanian VAT on every EU consumer sale and **no OSS registration**. For a
     business customer in another Member State it is the customer's country under Article
     44, invoiced under the reverse charge, which clause 3.3 already says. For a consumer
     established outside the EU, consultancy services fall outside the scope under Article
     59. This is simpler than the education route, which is the other reason to keep the
     description honest.
   - **The 2025 virtual-attendance rule does not bite.** Directive (EU) 2022/542 moved
     virtually attended **educational and similar events** to the customer's country from 1
     January 2025. It applies to an event someone attends, not to advice on their own
     project. If we ever describe the Checkpoint as a course or a class, that rule and the
     OSS registration come back.
   - **No training exemption either.** Article 132 exempts vocational training; advisory
     services are taxable. Do not budget for an exemption.
   - **Registration in Lithuania.** The €45,000 threshold for our own turnover.
   - **Services received from advisors abroad.** Fees invoiced to us by advisors in other EU
     states put us under the reverse charge and oblige us to register as a VAT payer for
     that purpose regardless of turnover.
   - Ask the accountant to confirm the classification in writing before the first invoice.
     It decides the rate, the registrations and the wording on the invoice.
5. **Why the Checkpoint is not sold through Paddle — checked 9 September 2026.** Kept here
   so the decision is not re-opened by someone who did not see the reason. Paddle's
   Acceptable Use Policy prohibits "human services that are not related to a software
   offering", naming pure consulting, advisory services, coaching and access to a community
   of experts, and separately prohibits offerings where no bona fide software is sold. A
   Checkpoint is four live 45-minute calls with a human curator, and — see point 4 — it is
   advisory work on the team's own project rather than a taught course, which is precisely
   the category Paddle names. Calling it a course does
   not fix it: the AUP's restriction on courses is about owning the material, which we do,
   but the substance Paddle looks at is live human delivery. The risk is not a refusal at
   sign-up, it is held funds after launch. An **AI-curator** Checkpoint has no live calls
   and would pass on any reading, but there is no reason to split billing between two
   providers.
6. **Refund charges.** Neither Paddle nor Stripe returns its processing fee when a refund
   is issued, so clause 4.1's deduction is real. Note it can never apply to a consumer's
   statutory withdrawal — days 1-7 fall inside the 14-day window, so a buyer who words the
   request as a withdrawal is refunded in full either way, and the deduction realistically
   bites only on business purchases. Chargebacks now land on us rather than on a merchant
   of record: budget for the fee and for the evidence work.
   - Sources: Paddle AUP, <https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle>;
     Paddle refund policy, <https://www.paddle.com/legal/refund-policy>; Paddle business
     verification, <https://www.paddle.com/help/start/account-verification/what-is-business-verification>.
7. **Volunteering.** The formalities under the Law on Volunteering: what a volunteering
   agreement must contain, the tax-free limits on expense reimbursement, and whether
   accident insurance for volunteers is expected of the organiser — and whether free
   consultations may sit inside the one Advisor Agreement (GDHUB-102) or the volunteering
   has to be its own agreement; the draft offers one on request (4.6).
8. **The withdrawal checkbox.** Days 8-14 are covered only if the buyer expressly requests
   immediate performance and acknowledges losing the right once fully performed, at
   checkout, as a separate affirmative action. Without it the full 14-day right survives
   and a proportionate deduction cannot be applied. Since GDHUB-103 the request is 5.2 of
   the Purchase Terms with its own checkbox, citing Articles 8(8), 14(3) and 16(a) of
   Directive 2011/83/EU; confirm that a box of its own is enough without a document of its
   own, and give the Civil Code articles to cite in place of "the corresponding provisions".
8a. **The withdrawal function, Article 11a.** Directive 2023/2673 added to 2011/83/EU a
   duty for contracts concluded online: a "withdraw from contract here" function, available
   for the whole withdrawal period, with a confirmation step and an acknowledgement on a
   durable medium. The Purchase Terms (5.2) still ask the buyer to write an email, and the
   app has no such button. Confirm the date it applies from in Lithuania and how it was
   transposed; the button itself is product work.
9. **Non-circumvention.** Whether six months against taking an author off-platform is
   proportionate under Lithuanian and EU competition rules for an NGO of this size.
10. **P2B Regulation (EU) 2019/1150.** Whether paid consultations, where advisors set their
   own price and are listed in a catalogue, make us an online intermediation service. The
   draft already carries the 15-day notice of change and the statement of reasons on
   suspension. If the regulation applies, the catalogue also needs published ranking
   parameters.
11. **Reviews.** The Omnibus rules require saying how ratings are verified. The advisor
   profile shows ratings, so the Terms of Use need a line about it.
12. **Lithuanian versions.** Both agreements are English. Consumers resident in Lithuania
    are entitled to Lithuanian; the drafts already say the Lithuanian version prevails for
    them, which means it has to exist before launch.

## Product decisions taken in the drafts

Stated so they are visible, not buried:

- **The Platform is the seller, the curator is engaged by us.** The author's counterparty
  is the VšĮ, not the curator. This follows the Checkpoint Standard, where we set the
  price, replace a curator and refund the author.
- **One agreement for the whole advisor role** — volunteer advice, paid consultations,
  curation — activated by enabling the Advisor Space.
- **Refunds:** full within 7 days, less payment charges we do not get back; days 8-14
  under the statutory right, less the value of what was delivered and nothing else;
  nothing after day 14 except our own failure to deliver.
- **The Checkpoint is billed on the VšĮ's Stripe account**, so the Platform is the seller
  of record and issues its own invoices. Clauses 3.2, 3.3 and 4.6 of the Checkpoint terms
  say so. Paddle keeps the products it already carries.
- **A Checkpoint is advisory work, not a course.** The team works on its own project and is
  advised on it; there is no syllabus and nothing is taught. The published texts describe it
  that way, and the VAT treatment in point 4 rests on it, so marketing copy should not drift
  into "course", "training" or "class".
- **One seller for the author: the VšĮ.** The program is run with people — curators,
  calls, a verdict — and that is the Lithuanian organisation's side of the house, so it
  sells the whole thing, software part included. Whatever the founder's entity sells in its
  own name on the platform layer — vacancies, subscriptions — keeps its own rail and is not
  described here. This is also why the AI-curator Checkpoint is not routed through Paddle:
  it would put a second seller, in another jurisdiction, in front of the same buyer for one
  variant of one product.
- **Acceptance is recorded** by version and timestamp. Every version has to stay
  retrievable — a running Checkpoint continues under the version in force when it started.
