# Platform Licence and Services Agreement — draft for counsel

Internal draft, not a published document and not in the legal registry. Written
20 October 2026 as the outline a Lithuanian lawyer turns into the signed agreement. Square
brackets are values to fill or choices to make; **Notes for counsel** at the end list what
must be checked. Background: [entity-jurisdiction-research.md](entity-jurisdiction-research.md).

**Purpose in one line:** the Licensee operates gdhub.io and sells through it; the Licensor
keeps everything the platform is made of, is paid for its use, and can move the platform
to another operator on notice.

## 1. Definitions

- **Platform** — the software at gdhub.io and its subdomains: source code, database
  schema, design, texts of the programmes (the Checkpoint standard, the curator handbook,
  the program pages), documentation, and every later version.
- **Marks** — the name "GDHub" / "Game Developers Hub", the logo, and any registered or
  unregistered sign used for the Platform.
- **Domain** — gdhub.io and every domain pointing to the Platform.
- **Infrastructure** — the accounts the Platform runs on: source code hosting, application
  hosting, database, storage, email, translation, video calls, AI providers, analytics.
- **Operator Assets** — what the Licensee holds as the seller: customer contracts, its
  payment processor accounts (Stripe), its bank accounts, invoices, tax records, curator
  and expert contracts.
- **User Data** — personal data of Platform users processed for the Services.
- **Services** — what the Licensee sells through the Platform: Checkpoints, paid
  consultations, and any service added by written agreement.
- **Net Revenue** — what users paid the Licensee for the Services in a month, less VAT,
  refunds and chargebacks, payment processor fees, and fees paid to curators and experts.
- **Successor** — the operator the Licensor names under Section 11.

## 2. What is granted

1. The Licensor grants the Licensee a **non-exclusive, non-transferable,
   non-sublicensable** licence to use the Platform, the Marks and the Domain **solely to
   operate the Platform and sell the Services** under this Agreement, worldwide except
   where sanctions forbid it.
2. Nothing is assigned. The Platform, the Marks, the Domain and the Infrastructure stay
   the Licensor's. The Licensee does not register, in any country, a mark, domain or company
   name containing "GDHub" or a confusingly similar sign.
3. **Improvements belong to the Licensor.** Anything the Licensee or its contractors create
   for the Platform — code, designs, texts, programme rules — is assigned to the Licensor on
   creation, and the Licensee obtains that assignment from its contractors in writing.
4. The Licensor may license the Platform to others, including for other regions ("hubs").

## 3. Who does what

1. **The Licensor** hosts, maintains and develops the Platform and keeps the
   Infrastructure in its own name and at its own cost [or: cost recharged under Section 6].
   It gives the Licensee the administrative access it needs to operate.
2. **The Licensee** is the seller and the contracting party with users: it concludes and
   performs the customer contracts, holds the Stripe account, invoices, accounts for VAT,
   pays curators and experts, handles complaints, refunds and disputes, and complies with
   consumer law.
3. The Licensee may not move the Platform, its code or its data to infrastructure it
   controls, or copy them, except for its own legal records.

## 4. Data protection

1. The Licensee is the **controller** of User Data. The Licensor, which hosts the
   Platform, is its **processor** under a data processing agreement under GDPR Article 28
   annexed as Schedule 2 (sub-processors: the Infrastructure providers).
2. The privacy policy names the Licensee as controller and states that the Platform may be
   transferred to another operator, who would then become controller (Section 11).

## 5. Payments processing

1. The Licensee keeps its Stripe account in good standing and connected to the Platform
   through keys the Licensee provides, rotated on request.
2. The Licensee does **not** store users' payment methods for future charges in its Stripe
   account unless the Licensor agrees in writing, so that nothing of value to users is
   locked in an account that cannot be transferred.

## 6. Fee

1. The Licensee pays the Licensor **[X]% of Net Revenue** each month [alternatively: a fixed
   monthly fee of [amount], or the greater of the two].
2. The fee is invoiced by the Licensor after the end of each month on the Licensee's
   monthly statement (Section 7) and paid within **15 days** of the invoice, by bank
   transfer to the Licensor's account in [Georgia], in [USD/EUR].
3. The fee is stated exclusive of VAT. The Licensee accounts for any VAT due on the services
   it receives under the reverse charge.
4. If the law requires the Licensee to withhold tax on the fee, it withholds at the rate
   reduced by the applicable double tax treaty, provided the Licensor has given a
   certificate of tax residence, and hands the Licensor the proof of payment.
5. Late payment bears interest at [the statutory rate].

## 7. Reports and audit

1. Within **5 working days** after each month the Licensee sends a statement of gross
   receipts, refunds, chargebacks, processor fees, curator and expert fees, VAT and Net
   Revenue, reconciled with the Stripe payout reports.
2. The Licensor has read-only access to the Stripe account's reports [Stripe team role
   "View only"] and may, once a year at its cost, have the statements checked by an
   accountant of its choice.

## 8. Brand

1. The Licensee uses the Marks only as the Licensor's brand guidelines allow and only for
   the Platform. Every published document names the Licensee as operator and the Licensor
   as owner of the Platform (Terms of Use §10).

## 9. Sanctions and compliance

1. Each party warrants that it, its owners and its managers are not designated under EU,
   UN, US or UK sanctions, and informs the other at once if that changes.
2. The Licensee does not sell to persons in Belarus or Russia, or to designated persons,
   and keeps the sales restrictions configured on the Platform.
3. Either party may suspend performance to the extent needed to comply with sanctions law.

## 10. Term and termination

1. The Agreement runs for an indefinite term from [date].
2. **The Licensor may terminate on [60] days' written notice, without giving reasons.**
3. The Licensee may terminate on [90] days' written notice.
4. Either party may terminate at once by written notice if the other materially breaches
   and does not cure within [15] days, becomes insolvent, or becomes subject to sanctions.
   Non-payment of the fee for more than [30] days is a material breach.
5. **Change of control:** a change in the Licensee's founder (dalininkas), its director, or
   its articles that affects its activity on the Platform is notified in advance and lets
   the Licensor terminate at once.

## 11. Transition to a Successor

From the notice of termination the Licensee:

1. stops selling new Services from the date the Licensor names, and completes those already
   sold, or hands them to the Successor where the user agrees;
2. keeps its Stripe account open for [120] days after the end of the Agreement to complete
   holds, captures, refunds and disputes, and handles them in good faith;
3. transfers to the Successor, at the Licensor's request, the User Data and the customer,
   curator and expert contracts to the extent the law allows, informs users of the change of
   operator in the wording the Licensor provides, and gives them the chance to object;
4. hands over the records the Successor needs to perform the running contracts;
5. stops using the Marks and the Domain on the end date, and removes itself as operator from
   every published document;
6. is paid the fee due up to the end date and owes nothing further, except obligations under
   this Section.

The Licensor may switch the Platform's operator configuration and the Domain to the
Successor on the end date; the Licensee consents to it now.

## 12. Non-solicitation

For [12] months after the end, the Licensee does not run a competing platform for game
developers built from the Platform, its texts or its programme rules [and does not solicit
the Platform's curators to such a platform].

## 13. Liability

1. The Licensor provides the Platform as it is and is liable only for intent and gross
   negligence, capped at the fees paid in the [12] months before the claim.
2. The Licensee is liable to users as the seller and indemnifies the Licensor for claims
   arising from the Services, its contracts and its compliance.

## 14. Confidentiality, law, disputes

1. Both keep the terms, revenue figures and non-public information confidential.
2. Governed by the law of the Republic of Lithuania. Disputes go to [Vilnius courts /
   Vilnius Court of Commercial Arbitration], in English.
3. Changes only in writing. The Agreement may be signed with qualified electronic

## Notes for counsel

1. **Non-profit fit.** Is paying a percentage of Net Revenue to an outside licensor
   compatible with the VšĮ's statutory goals and the ban on profit distribution? What
   makes the rate "market" — a benchmark of SaaS / platform licences?
2. **Royalty or service fee — decides tax on both sides.** In Georgia, royalties fall
   outside the Licensor's 1% small business status (Resolution No. 415, Annex 5; Tax Code
   Art. 90) and are taxed under the general rules, about 20%; software development and IT
   services stay inside it, consulting does not. In Lithuania, royalties paid abroad carry
   withholding tax, service fees usually do not. So the fee should be drafted, and
   performed, as payment for **platform services** — hosting, operation, development under
   Section 3.1 — with the brand and code licence as an incidental part of that service
   rather than a separately priced royalty. Confirm the split holds on substance in both
   countries, and the Lithuania–Georgia treaty rate if any part stays a royalty.
3. **VAT** on the fee for the Licensee (reverse charge, registration).
4. **Data transfer on termination.** Whether moving users to a Successor needs their consent
   or only notice with a right to object; what the privacy policy must say now for that to
   hold.
5. **Assigning consumer contracts.** Running Checkpoints can be assigned only with the
   consumer's consent — confirm, and whether completing them under the Licensee is the safer
   default.
6. **Termination without cause** by the Licensor: enforceable as drafted, or does Lithuanian
   law require reasons or compensation for an indefinite-term contract?
7. **Change of control** clause against a VšĮ: workable, given the founder can change the
   articles?
8. **Sanctions:** payments to a Georgian entrepreneur who is a Belarusian citizen resident in
   Belarus — any restriction under Regulation 765/2006 on the Lithuanian side, and what the
   Licensee's bank will ask.
9. **Belarusian side** (separate adviser): how the Licensor's income through the Georgian
   entity is declared in Belarus, and whether operating the Platform from Belarus makes a
   permanent establishment of the Georgian entity there.
