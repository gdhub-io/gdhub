## GDHub Checkpoint

**Every 28 days, one step closer to releasing your game.**

This program puts structure around 28 days of work on one stage of a project: a finish
date nobody moves; a goal agreed with the curator on day 1; a devlog every week that
somebody actually reads; a one-click check-in from every member; two reviews, on day 14
and day 21; and a verdict at the end.

Participants will complete one agreed goal across 4 weeks of their own production. On
day 1 the author and the curator write the goal card: what the project has now, and what
must exist at the end. Across those 4 weeks, the team will complete tasks including
publishing a devlog every week, attaching an artifact to every line of the outcome list
— a document, images, a video or a build link — cutting the scope on day 14, and showing
the result at the shared gathering of the season.

By completing the checkpoint, participants will have carried one stage of their game to a
date they did not set for themselves, with evidence behind every outcome and a written
verdict on the record of the project and of everyone who worked on it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Level** | Any project stage, from concept to release |
| **Prerequisites** | A project on GDHub and a goal reachable in 28 days |
| **Rhythm** | 28 days · a devlog and a check-in every week · four calls of 45 minutes · one showing |
| **Team size** | Author plus up to 8; author and up to 4 on a call |
| **Curator** | A named specialist for the stage you are on — or the AI curator (soon) |
| **Verdict** | Written by day 30: goal met and stage closed, goal met and stage open, or goal missed |

## The program

| Day | What happens | Whose move |
|---|---|---|
| before | You describe the project and what already exists. The curator takes the checkpoint. | Author, curator |
| **1** | **Consultation 1 — the goal.** Together with the curator you set a goal that fits 28 days, and write down what must exist at the end. | Curator leads |
| 6 | Devlog for week one. What got done, what is next, what is in the way. | Author |
| **7** | **Consultation 2 — risks.** What is already in the way, and what to do about it. | Curator leads |
| 13 | Devlog for week two. | Author |
| **14** | **Consultation 3 — scope.** The one day the goal may change, so it can still be reached. | Curator leads |
| 20 | Devlog for week three. | Author |
| **21** | **Consultation 4 — the run-in.** What has to be ready for the gathering. | Curator leads |
| 26 | Every artifact attached. An outcome with nothing against it counts as missed. | Author |
| 27 | Devlog for week four. | Author |
| **28** | **Shared gathering.** Your team shows what it has, beside the other teams of the season. | Platform runs it |
| **by 30** | **The verdict.** The result of the checkpoint in writing, with the reasoning behind it. | Curator |

A call runs 45 minutes whatever the size of the team and opens with the author saying how
it is going. The devlog is published at least 24 hours before its call: without it the
call moves once, and the second time it is gone. Either side may move one call per
checkpoint with 12 hours' notice. There is no extension — 28 days always end in a verdict,
and every verdict leads to a new checkpoint, bought separately.

### What the author brings

- A project on GDHub and a team roster fixed before day 1 — it does not change inside a checkpoint
- Four devlogs, each published 24 hours before its call
- One artifact per outcome line: a document, images, a video or a build link
- The author and up to four of the team at every call
- One click a week from each member confirming they are still on the project

### What a checkpoint is not

- Doing the work — code, art, text — or line-by-line code review
- Marketing the game, introductions to publishers, legal advice
- Consultations beyond the five, or the whole team on a call when it is larger than five
- Any promise that the goal is met: the curator runs the checkpoint, not the game

them, and an affordable AI curator. Choose by what you need and what your budget allows.
