# Checkpoint Curator Handbook

What a curator does, on which day, and what the platform holds them to. Source of the
published page: <https://claude.ai/code/artifact/93c0fa2b-1075-44e6-86ea-f74f101f186e>

Split out of [the author's info sheet](checkpoint-info-sheet.md) so neither document
speaks to two audiences at once. The binding rules are in
[docs/checkpoint-standard.md](../checkpoint-standard.md); this file is how they are
handed to a curator.

---

## The role

A checkpoint is 28 days of work on one stage of one project. The author buys it, does the
work and publishes the devlogs. **You run it:** you make the goal reachable on day 1,
answer every devlog in writing, say on day 14 what is not going to fit, and write the
verdict by day 30.

You are not hired to make the game. No code, no art, no line-by-line review, no
introductions to publishers — and no promise that the goal is met. What you owe is
judgement, on time, in writing.

**Take only as many projects as you can run without missing a call.** Two missed calls in
a row and the platform replaces you inside the checkpoint and returns the author's money
for what did not happen.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Your time** | About 7.5 hours per checkpoint for a solo author, plus 0.5 for each member of the team — two to three hours a week |
| **Calls** | Four of 45 minutes, plus the season's shared gathering |
| **Writing** | An answer to each of the four devlogs, the goal card and the result card |
| **Hard deadline** | The verdict on the platform by day 30 |
| **Your rate** | Set by your level; the platform adds its fee on top |

## The 28 days, your moves

| Day | Your move |
|---|---|
| before | Read what the author wrote about the project and what already exists, then take the checkpoint. |
| **1** | **Consultation 1 — the goal.** Read the outcome list the author bought with: it is a draft, not the card. Say plainly what will not fit in 28 days and cut it with them — a first-time author who wrote "a finished game" leaves with a slice, a loop, one level. Then fix the card: what exists now with its proof, what must exist at the end with an empty slot per line, the goal in one sentence. What was bought stays readable beside it. |
| 6 | Devlog for week one. Read it before the call and answer it in writing. |
| **7** | **Consultation 2 — risks.** Let the author speak first — ten minutes at most; you have read the devlog, so this is a check, not a report from scratch. Name what is already in the way and what to do about it while three weeks remain. |
| 13 | Devlog for week two. |
| **14** | **Consultation 3 — scope.** Say plainly what is not going to fit and what to cut; this is the day you are paid for. A line may come off, be reworded into the smaller promise it has become, or be added when the team is ahead — and nothing else changes after today. Every change is recorded beside the day-1 wording, never over it. |
| 20 | Devlog for week three. |
| **21** | **Consultation 4 — the run-in.** Look at the state of the work and settle what has to be ready for the gathering. Push for artifacts to be attached as they land; everything is due by day 26. |
| 27 | Devlog for week four. |
| **28** | **Shared gathering — consultation 5.** Every team of the season speaks in turn. Answer each of your own teams out loud. The verdict is not announced here. |
| **by 30** | **The verdict.** Write the result card: what of the list is done and what proves it, what was cut, the verdict and the reasoning. Goal met and stage closed · goal met, stage open · goal missed. Every verdict leads to a new checkpoint. |

You may count an outcome no artifact covers — playtest notes, a conversation with a
publisher, something shown live on the call — and write one line saying why. It stands
beside that outcome for the author to read.

## Rules

| | |
|---|---|
| **Devlog before the call** | No devlog 24 hours before a call and the call moves once per checkpoint. The second time it is gone and is not returned. |
| **Moving a call** | Either side may move one call per checkpoint, with at least 12 hours' notice. |
| **The goal is agreed, then binding** | What the author wrote when buying is a draft; consultation 1 turns it into the card the verdict is measured against. Cutting it there is you doing your job, not a failure. |
| **After that it changes once** | Only on consultation 3, and every change is recorded with the day-1 wording kept beside it. |
| **What is not attached is not done** | An outcome with nothing against it on day 28 counts as missed — unless you count it yourself and write why. |
| **Silence is not a verdict** | No verdict by day 30 and an administrator closes the checkpoint; money for consultations that did not happen goes back. |
| **A curator who disappears** | Two missed calls in a row and the platform replaces you inside the checkpoint and returns the money for what did not happen. |
| **An author who disappears** | Two missed calls in a row with no devlogs and the checkpoint closes as goal missed. No refund. |
| **Who is on the call** | The author and up to four of the team. The rest read your written answer to the devlog. The call stays 45 minutes whatever the size of the team. |
| **The team roster** | Changes between checkpoints, not inside one. Someone joining mid-checkpoint is paid for from the next one. |

### What is recorded about you

- The projects you ran and how many checkpoints
- How many closed as goal met, how many led to one more checkpoint, how many missed
- The authors' rating with the number of people who gave one — no average appears until five have answered

### Not your job

- Doing the work — code, art, text — or line-by-line code review
- Marketing the game, introductions to publishers, legal advice
- Consultations beyond the five, or the whole team on a call when it is larger than five
- Promising the goal is met
